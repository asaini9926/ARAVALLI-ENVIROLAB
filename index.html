      Regulatory Guidance

      CTE vs CTO: Understand what your plant needs.

      Under the Water Act (1974) and Air Act (1981), state boards grant pollution consent in two mandatory stages.

      Parameter Consent to Establish (CTE) Consent to Operate (CTO)
      When NeededPrior to construction, installation or plant expansionAfter completion, prior to production or commissioning
      Board FocusSite suitability, zoning, control system designInstalled machinery, test reports & operational compliance
      Turnaround30 to 45 business days30 to 45 business days
      Validity PeriodValid during construction (usually 3–5 years)1 to 5 years (renewable based on category)

      Red Category

      High pollution potential. Cement, chemicals, distilleries, electroplating. Requires strictest documentation and monitoring.

      Orange Category

      Moderate potential. Stone crushers, dairy, hotels, real estate towers, automotive fabrication.

      Green Category

      Low potential. Food processing, garment assembly, dry packaging. Faster expedited approval cycle.

      White Category

      Practically non-polluting. Solar power parks, wind units, handlooms. Requires only intimation / exemption.

      Required for CTE

      • Detailed Project Report (DPR) & Cost Proof
      • Site Master Plan & Land Title / RIICO Lease
      • Proposed ETP / STP / APCD Schematics

      Required for CTO Renewal

      • Copy of existing CTE / CTO Order
      • Certified Air, Stack & Effluent Test Reports
      • Annual Hazardous Waste Return (Form 4)
